## H1 Paper 1 (8834/01), 11 Sep 2024
**Q1 Fieldwork – Consumer perception of community response to CC.** 4 × 17-yr-olds. Hypothesis: 'Consumers in Singapore see a stronger role for community response than for government and businesses in tackling climate change.' Community response = action by non-governmental & non-corporate stakeholders. Online survey via friends (snowball) in May; target 100 not reached → surveyed individually outside Bugis Junction, Wed & Thu afternoon; face-to-face interviews with 6 family/neighbours (incl. 2 elderly); secondary data NCCS 2019 perception survey. R1 street-level views outside Bugis Junction; R2 consumers' perception (Govt/Business/Community/NGOs "have a part to play", Likert 1–5); R3 NCCS 2019 findings.
- (a) Explain why the hypothesis is appropriate. [3]
- (b) With ref to R1, suggest challenges students may face conducting the survey online and at Bugis Junction. [4]
- (c) With ref to R1, suggest how students could minimise potential risks at Bugis Junction. [5] – afternoon heat, carpark crossings, alone.
- (d) Explain the ethical considerations when interviewing family members and neighbours. [4]
- (e) Compare consumers' perception of actors' importance in fighting CC (R2). [4] – Business 71% top-2; Govt highest SA 38%.
- (f) Students concluded consumers think community response more important than govt and businesses. With ref to R1, R2, R3, evaluate validity. [10]

**Q2 Urban growth, shrinking cities, waste.** R4 urban growth in selected cities 1950–2025 (Tokyo, NY, London, Lagos, Mumbai/Bombay, Cairo); R5 annual growth rates primary cities vs suburbs, NY & Boston metro 2010–2020; R6 Deonar dumping ground fire, Mumbai + location in MMR; R7 waste production Mumbai vs Sweden + Tekniska Verken (Swedish multi-utility energy co., waste→biogas/heat/electricity; Mumbai 5,025 t mixed waste).
- (a) Cite data to compare urban growth in selected cities (R4). [3]
- (b) With ref to R4, explain how demand may be placed on natural environments in Lagos and Mumbai. [4]
- (c) With ref to R5, suggest possible challenges faced by New York and Boston in progressing towards SUD. [4] – urban population loss, dereliction, brownfields, tax base.
- (d) With ref to R6, explain limitations of landfills as a waste management strategy. [6]
- (e) With ref to R7, suggest how less demand is placed on the environment when waste is viewed as a potential resource. [5]

**Q3 Climate change & ecosystems.** R8 global surface temperature change 1880–2020, annual average vs natural drivers only (IPCC AR6); R9 effects of major drivers (habitat change, CC, etc.) on biodiversity by ecosystem (MA table); R10 tipping points of Earth's climate systems (PIK).
- (a) Cite data from R8 to describe changes in global surface temp for annual average and natural drivers only 1880–2020. [4]
- (b) With ref to R9, compare effect of habitat change and CC on biodiversity in ecosystems. [5]
- (c) Explain how ecosystems may be affected by contemporary CC. [4] – coral bleaching, acidification, range shifts, phenology.
- (d) With ref to R10, describe main effects of global warming on Earth's climate systems. [4]
- (e) With ref to R8, R9, R10, suggest how changes to aquatic and terrestrial ecosystems can impact humans. [5]
