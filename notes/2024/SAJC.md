## H1 Paper 1 (8834/01), 22 Aug 2024 — no answer scheme
**Q1 Fieldwork – East Coast Park & elderly of Marine Parade.** 8 JC1 H2 students; June hols, one weekday 8am–9pm; 6 sites over 15 km park (Marina East Desalination Plant Zone A → Zone H, 2h30 walk). Hypothesis: 'East Coast Park meets the needs of the elderly.' Questionnaire to elderly encountered, target 20 per site; photos + observations. R1 map with 6 sample sites; R2 photos of 4 sites (Raintree Cove, East Coast Lagoon Food Village, Amber Beacon, Marina East Desalination Plant); R3 survey results (frequency; activities; access 40% very easy; facilities ratings: seating, restrooms, paths, signage; safety 30/45/15/5/5; comfort; suggestions: shade 40%, seating 35%, restrooms 30%).
- (a) Explain the strengths and weaknesses of the hypothesis. [4]
- (b) With ref to R1 & R2, explain possible considerations in choosing the sampling sites. [3]
- (c) With ref to R2, suggest how students could minimise potential risks at the four sample sites. [4]
- (d) Explain why it is good for students to take photos and record observations at each site. [4]
- (e) Using R3, describe how East Coast Park meets the needs of the elderly. [5]
- (f) Evaluate the students' investigation into the extent East Coast Park meets the needs of the elderly living in Marine Parade. [10]

- (b) Explain strengths and limitations of the data representation in R1. [4] – cartogram.
- (c) Explain the impact of technology on provisioning resources. [4]
- (d) Citing data from R2A, describe the distribution of hydropower dams along the Mekong. [4]
- (e) With ref to R2A & R2B, explain the impacts of developing hydropower along the Mekong. [8] – arable land flooded, Tonle Sap flow, Vietnam rice, Laos energy, Luang Prabang UNESCO heritage.

**Q2 ENSO, meanders, weathering (Cluster 2).** R3 neutral ENSO phase (Walker circulation); R4 El Niño phase; R5 photo section of a fluvial landform (meander, A-B cross-section); R6 climograph of a tropical desert (BWh; MAT 18.9°C, 203 mm, ATR 6°C).
- (a) Distinguish between the two ENSO phases (R3, R4). [4]
- (b) Using R4, explain how atmospheric circulation influences rainfall patterns in the tropics. [5]
- (c) Draw a cross section along A-B and label main features of the fluvial landform (R5). [5] – river cliff, point bar.

- (e) With ref to R6, explain what makes physical weathering more effective in the arid tropics than the humid tropics. [5] – diurnal range, exfoliation, salt weathering.
- (f) Explain how rock composition determines the rate of both physical and chemical weathering. [5] – dark minerals, granite (feldspar, mica, quartz).

## H2 Paper 2 (9173/02), 12 Sep 2024
**Q1 Fieldwork – Flood risk, Kallang River, Bishan-AMK Park.** 8 students, 2 teams; Hypothesis: 'Site B has a higher flood risk than Site A.' R1 map (A→B 2.7 km); R2 photos Sites A (concrete, railing) & B (naturalised); R3 velocity data (A avg 0.092 m/s; B 0.073 m/s; 11am/1pm/2pm, 2 readings each).
- (a) With help of R1 & R2, explain two strengths and two limitations of the hypothesis. [4]
- (b) With ref to R1 & R2, suggest how students can minimise three potential risks during data collection. [6]
- (c) Explain the steps involved in determining the velocity at Site B (R2). [8]
- (d) With ref to R3, explain the considerations students might have had when designing the data collection sheet. [5]
- (e) Sketch two line graphs to represent velocity at Sites A and B over time (R3). [5]
- (f) Explain how the data in R3 proves the students' hypothesis. [2]
- (g) Evaluate the extent to which the fieldwork was useful in understanding flood risk along the Kallang River. [10]
