## H1 Paper 1 (8834/01), 2024
**Q1 Fieldwork – Ageing well, Winterthur, Switzerland.** 4 Zurich University students; 3 elements for ageing well: "sense of well-being", "having good physical health", "preserving good mental health". Survey of 56 participants (convenience sampling); 10 chosen for face-to-face interviews in familiar settings/homes, some in pairs on request; voice recorder. R1 participant profile (women 53/men 3; married 41, widowed 12; disease 44; assisted aid 32; fitness ratings); R2 survey recording sheet; R3 results Q1 "most important to age well" (physical health 50%), Q2 "who can help you age well".
- (a) Suggest a possible hypothesis and explain why it is appropriate. [3]
- (b) Explain two criteria students could use to select participants. [4]
- (c) With ref to R2, suggest how the survey recording sheet can be improved. [4]
- (d) Explain the strengths of the students' approach to the interviews. [5] – mother tongue, familiar settings, voice recorder.
- (e) Explain how students could minimise two potential ethical issues. [4]
- (f) Students concluded elderly in Winterthur perceived good physical health as most important for ageing well. Evaluate validity. [10]

**Q2 Ecological footprint, slums, waste – Mthatha, South Africa.** R4 Earth Overshoot Day 1971 (25 Dec) → 2024 (1 Aug); R5 waste disposed by settlement type, Mthatha (food waste 46.9%/70.5%; disposable nappies 2nd); R6 map of settlement types + 18 illegal dumpsites (SA HDI 0.71, 67% urban).

- (b) With ref to R4, describe change in arrival of Earth Overshoot Day 1971–2024. [5] – use "earlier/later", not "fell/rose".
- (c) With ref to R5, compare waste disposed by informal slum settlements and low-density residential settlements. [4]
- (d) Explain reasons for development of slums in urban areas of countries at low levels of development. [4]
- (e) With ref to R5 & R6, explain how Mthatha's challenges in managing NHSW can limit its progress towards SUD. [6] – political, social (NIMBY, nappies), economic, environmental.

**Q3 Carbon inequality, carbon cycle, responses.** R7 % CO2 from individual consumption by income decile (richest 10% = 49%; poorest 50% = 10%); R8 global carbon cycle perturbation 2011–2020 (fossil 35 Gt CO2/yr, land use 5; vegetation uptake 11, ocean 10, atmosphere +19); R9 Singapore carbon tax (S$50–80/t by 2030).

## H2 Paper 2 (9173/02), 2024
**Q1 Fieldwork – Flood risk, Edogawa City, Tokyo.** 4 × 18-year-olds, 4–7 June; >695,000 people; between Arakawa (W) and Edo (E) rivers. Used city flood hazard map to pick 4 sites; planned velocity/width/depth (50 m tape, 2×10 m twine, metre rule, stopwatch, 5 oranges, ranging poles) but could not → used online river data; surveys 25 people/site, one site per day 2–4pm, systematic sampling, mix of ages/genders. R1 flood hazard map + 4 sites; R2 ground view (fences, permits needed for water activities); R3 online river data (Site 1 v 0.25, w 650, d 12; Site 2 0.07/150/15; Site 3 0.1/250/11; Site 4 0.2/150/11); R4 questionnaire (in English; Q5 jargon); R5 Q3 flood frequency tallies by site.
- (a) With ref to R1, suggest a suitable hypothesis and explain why it is suitable. [3]
- (b) With ref to R1, suggest reasons why the students chose these four sites. [4]
- (c) Using R2 & R3 only, suggest why it was not feasible to take river measurements. [5]
- (d) Describe the steps students could have taken to measure river velocity with the given equipment. [6]
- (e) Using R4, explain strengths and weaknesses of the questionnaire. [6]
- (f) Using a suitable data representation method, present results to Q3 (R5) and justify your choice. [6]
- (g) Students concluded Site 1 has the highest flood risk. With ref to all resources, evaluate validity. [10] – risk = hazard × vulnerability × exposure; discharge 1,950 m³/s at Site 1.
