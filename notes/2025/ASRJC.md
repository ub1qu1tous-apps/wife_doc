## H1 Paper 1 (8834/01), 25 Aug 2025
**Q1 Fieldwork – Park visits & elderly mental well-being, Kim Pong Park, Tiong Bahru.** 8 students, Saturday morning in June 9–11am; cluster of 8 low-rise blocks (43–50); light rain earlier. Hypothesis: "Elderly residents who often visit neighbourhood parks tend to have better mental well-being." Group 1 (4 students) on-the-spot interviews at park exit with 10 elderly visitors; Group 2 (4) in-home interviews with 10 residents of Blks 49 & 50. R1 map + photos (Kim Pong Park SW of blocks across Moh Guan Ter/Kim Tian Rd; Blks 49–50 nearest; 4-storey walk-up; park exit sign); R2 questionnaire (Q1 visit frequency never/rarely, sometimes 1–2/wk, often 3–5, very often; Q2 main reason; Q3 mood after visit 1–5; Q4 reasons for not going; Q5 "Don't you agree that parks are the best way for seniors to feel less depressed?"; Q6 "Do you go to the park because you're lonely and have nothing else to do?"; Q7 "Do parks make old people happier?"); R3 table of 20 respondents (13 M/7 F) visits/week 0–7 vs mood 1–5 with reasons (e.g. 7 visits/mood 5 daily tai chi; 0 visits/mood 1 mosquito bites, loss of spouse, chronic health) – clear positive association.
- (a) Explain whether the students' hypothesis is suitable. [3] – small manageable site; testable via questionnaire; clear IV (visits) and DV (well-being).
- (b) With ref to R1, explain how each group could have best selected the respondents for their interviews. [4] – define 65+; G1 systematic every nth exiting elderly, adjust n to flow; G2 5 per block or proportional, every 3rd/4th door per floor; snowball as fallback.
- (c) With ref to R1, suggest how the students could minimise potential risks in conducting the survey. [3] – personal safety (pairs, well-lit areas, check-ins), emotional distress (avoid intrusive questions), weather/slippery paths after rain, hydration.
- (d) Explain the strengths and limitations of the survey questionnaire shown in R2. [5] – short closed questions, colour-gradient mood scale; Q5 leading, Q6 judgmental/embarrassing, Q7 "old people" offensive.
- (e) Sketch a graph to present "Visits per week" and "Mood Rating" in R3 and explain its suitability. [5] – scatter plot with title, labelled axes, trend line.
- (f) Evaluate the validity of the students' investigation. [10] – quantifiable consistent questions, familiar settings, qualitative reasons, clear trend; but n=20, one 2-hr Saturday, only 2 nearest blocks (over-represents visitors), young-old vs old-old lumped, biased questions, correlation ≠ causation (health/social networks).
