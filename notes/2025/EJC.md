**Q1 Fieldwork – Elderly liveability, Kampung Admiralty, Singapore.** 4 students; hypothesis: "Kampong Admiralty is a liveable neighbourhood that meets the social and psychological needs of the elderly." Questionnaire translated into English, Malay, Chinese, Tamil; one Friday in June hols, 1–2pm only; at the Community Plaza, approaching any passer-by who "looked like" 65+; 15 responses. R1 infographic of infrastructure/amenities in Kampung Admiralty Integrated Development (image-only); R2 survey questionnaire (image-only; SMG cites Q4 "easy to move around safely on your own?", Q8 "supports your sense of belonging and mental well-being?", Q9 open-ended); R3A Likert 1–5 results by age band (60–64 / 65–74 / 75+): Q4 move around safely = 4/4/7 respondents, mostly scores 2–3; Q7 opportunities for social interaction = 4/4/8 (75+: four gave 4); R3B Q9 quotes – A: stores expensive but attract young → intergenerational mingling; B: hawker food expensive, taste over health; C: medical & childcare centres "not that different" from elsewhere; D: ex-taxi driver offered only menial jobs (dishwashing, sweeping).
- (a) Explain why the students' hypothesis is suitable for investigation. [4] – suitable scale (one neighbourhood, 4 students); researchable via questionnaire; clearly defined (population, place, social & psychological dimensions).
- (b) Explain how the students can address possible issues related to research ethics when conducting their fieldwork. [4] – informed consent/opt out; confidentiality; accessible language/translations; short questionnaire, no jargon, time to respond; safe shaded seating; keep clear of pathways/noise.
- (c) With reference to Resource 2, suggest how the design of the questionnaire can be improved. - (d) Sketch a graph to represent the data for Question 4 shown in Resource 3A and give reasons why you have chosen that type of graph. [6] – clustered (grouped) bar chart; up to 4m for well-labelled sketch (title, axes, legend).

[10] – R1 facilities and some positive R3A scores support; but over half had mobility challenges, affordability concerns (R3B), survey ignores affordability/safety, n = 15; need data on affordability, night safety, those with greater physical/language barriers. ## H2 Paper 2 (9173/02), 17 Sep 2025
**Q1 Fieldwork – Flood risk & mitigation, Punggol Waterway (Site A) vs Kallang River (Site B), Singapore.** 4 students; hypothesis: 'The flood risk along Punggol Waterway is lower than the flood risk along Kallang River.' Methods: flood risk perception survey; bi-polar survey of success of flood mitigation strategies. Site A Tue 10 Dec, Site B Sun 15 Dec; in pairs, swapping methods. 10 Dec: every 10th resident met while walking around Site A, 9–10am → only 3 respondents. 15 Dec: every 5th resident, 9am–12pm at Site B; bi-polar pair finished by 10am and went back to Site A to survey 11am–12pm. R1A/1B photos of Sites A & B (image; A has riverside railings, B has sloping banks); R2 demographics of respondents (image; SMG: both mostly female, Site A ~4:1 F:M, Site B only slightly more F; Site A >⅔ aged 65+, 3 age groups; Site B mostly 35–65, all age groups); R3 Likert results – Site A (n = 14): Q1 concerned about flood risk 11/3/0/0/0 (1 = strongly disagree … 5 = strongly agree); Q4 aware of communication channels 11/2/1/0/0; Site B (n = 35): Q1 7/10/4/9/5; Q4 9/8/8/5/5; R4 bipolar sheet (+3 to −3): effective vs vulnerable to overtopping; enhances natural environment vs ugly; no limit vs prevents public access to river; no disturbance vs disturbs erosion/deposition; low vs high maintenance/operational cost; low-tech vs high-tech.
- (a) Explain one strength and one weakness in the students' hypothesis. [4] – strength: "flood risk" calculable (likelihood × severity); weakness: scale – entire rivers (Kallang runs Bishan-AMK Park to Marina Reservoir).
- (b) Explain how the sampling of respondents for the flood risk perception surveys could be improved. [5] – fixed stations instead of walking; stratified by age; consistent interval (keep every 10th).
- (c) With reference to Resources 1A and 1B, suggest how the students could minimise potential risks during data collection. [6] – sun (hats, sunblock); don't lean over railings/use devices (A); avoid bank slopes (B).
- (d) Compare the characteristics of the respondents in Sites A and B as shown in Resource 2. [5]
- (e) Explain how the data in Resource 3 may be represented using a suitable graphical method. [5] – compound (divided) bar graphs in %, one per question, sites on x-axis, graded colour legend.
- (f) Explain the limitations of the data collection sheet shown in Resource 4. [5] – positive on left is counter-intuitive; subjective descriptors ("ugly"); vague "comments"; low-tech = positive is questionable.
- (g) The students concluded that their hypothesis is true. Evaluate the validity of their conclusion. [10] – R3 Site A respondents less concerned; but skewed elderly sample, unequal n, flawed bipolar sheet, procedural issues (different days/times, changed interval).
