## H1 Paper 1 (8834/01), 29 Aug 2025
**Q1 Fieldwork – Food waste reduction initiatives, Bishan (Singapore).** 8 students; efforts = 24-hour community fridge + food recycling locker at Blk 112 Bishan St 12, periodic food rescue/redistribution events at Bishan CC. Hypothesis: "Residents who live nearer to community-based food waste initiatives are more likely to participate in them." Sat afternoon: interviewed 4 resident volunteers at Blk 112 & Bishan CC; following Sat: questionnaire to 40 residents at Bishan Bus Interchange. R1A map of study sites/efforts (OneMap); R1B photos (smart recycling boxes; void-deck community fridge/urban garden); R2 interview responses – 2A list of 7 volunteer roles (collection, distribution, digital app e.g. GoodHood.sg, social media ambassadors #BishanCC, fridge stewards, drivers, outreach), 2B quotes (elderly 67: "walk to the fridge every Saturday – church group restocks"; younger 25: "embarrassed taking food"; middle-aged 54: "prefer to compost at home. Those events are too public"), 2C screenshots of recruitment/Food Rescue posters (27 Nov 2024, Bishan CC); R3 survey form (demographics incl. block/street; awareness, how heard, participation Y/N, type, frequency; reasons top 2; future willingness); R4 summary results (participation Yes 72.5%; aware of fridge 65%, rescue events 52.5%, locker 37.5%, none 20%; heard via social media 37.5%, posters 22.5%; top reasons among 29 participants: environmental concern 62.1%, cost savings 48.3%, convenient location 37.9%; barriers among 11 non-participants: lack of time 54.5%, not interested 45.5%, not aware how 36.4%; **avg distance to Blk 112: participants 655.7 m vs non-participants 607.0 m; to Bishan CC 546.9 m vs 427.6 m**; 10% don't live in Bishan).
- (a) Explain why this hypothesis is appropriate for investigating food waste reduction. [4] – defined population & variable; neighbourhood scale manageable for 8 students/2 days; measurable (proximity vs behaviour); researchable (split into 2 groups of 4).
- (b) Suggest ethical considerations the group should take into account when collecting data on-site. [3] – confidentiality/anonymity (addresses), informed consent, don't disrupt live redistribution events.
- (c) With reference to Resource 2, describe how the data collected from the interviews can be analysed. [5] – categorical coding into themes (motivations/barriers: social bonding, embarrassment, privacy); compare demographic groups (elderly vs young); triangulate with digital screenshots (2C).
- (d) With reference to Resource 3, explain the strengths of the questionnaire used in this investigation. [4] – wide range of variables (demographic, spatial, behavioural, attitudinal); separates awareness (Q1) from participation (Q2); MCQ format → easy coding, "Others" allows qualitative; captures motivators & barriers.
- (e) Using Resource 4, sketch a graph to present top reasons for participation in local food waste reduction efforts. [4] – bar graph to scale; 62.1/48.3/37.9%; title, labelled axes, legend.
- (f) Students concluded that their hypothesis was proven wrong. With reference to all resources, evaluate the validity of the fieldwork. [10] – participants live *further* on average (R4) → contradicts hypothesis; awareness/motivation matter more than proximity; sequence (survey should precede interviews); location bias (all surveys at bus interchange); distance imprecise (street, not unit); vague terms ("occasionally", "cost savings"); add NEA/SEC secondary data.

**Q2 Ganga River – sediment, channel change, GBM delta (Cluster 2).** R5 schematic map of Ganga across alluvial Ganga Plain & Bengal Plain below Himalaya (Punjab Plain, Delhi, Brahmaputra Plain, Ganga Delta; ~23–28°N); R6 sediment grain size vs downstream distance 300–2,400 km (gravel/coarse sand only near 300 km; fine sand dominant ~400–2,000 km; silt & clay and very fine sand dominate beyond ~2,000 km); R7 maps of fluvial landform morphology near Munger, Bihar 1900, 1936, 1963 (river terraces, bars); R8 land cover GBM delta 2020 vs projected 2080 (cropland 55.6 → 43.2%; urban 17.9 → 30.2%; water 16.0 → 15.6%; wetland 8.0 → 8.5%; forest 1.6 → 1.5%; grassland 0.1 → 0.2%; bareland 0.8 → 0.8%).
- (a) With reference to Resources 5 and 6, account for the relationship between sediment size and downstream distance in the Ganga River. [4] – size decreases downstream (gravel/coarse sand upstream → fine sand by 400 km → silt/clay by 2,100 km); competence & Hjulström (coarse load settles first).
- (b) Using Resource 7, describe how the fluvial landforms of the Ganga River have changed between 1900 and 1963. - (c) With reference to Resources 5, 6 and 7, suggest factors that could influence changes in fluvial landforms of the Ganga River over time. - (f) Suggest the strengths and limitations of two strategies to manage fluvial floods. [4] – levees/embankments (quick, low-tech vs catastrophic breach, maintenance); dams (lower peaks vs rapid sedimentation in tropics).
