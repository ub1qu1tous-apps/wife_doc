**Q1 Fieldwork – Elderly well-being & public transport, Melbourne CBD, Australia.** 4 students; investigated whether Melbourne CBD supports well-being of the elderly, focusing on transportation access to social amenities. Hypothesis: "The quality and accessibility of Melbourne CBD's public transport network positively influence the social well-being of elderly people." 3 hours on a weekday afternoon; at Southern Cross Station surveyed 18 Caucasian elderly aged 67–84 (short questionnaire, closed + open-ended); boarded 4 trams around the CBD for observations (photos + checklist: lighting, reserved seating, wheelchair access, space). R1 map of CBD (tram routes, bus routes, dining & retail areas, Southern Cross Station at W edge; "8–10 minutes walk = 500 m" scale); R2 photos of Southern Cross Station (large road, unsheltered forecourt) and tram interior (narrow entrance, wheelchair user); R3 questionnaire (age, gender, years lived; Q4 commute frequency 5-point; Q5 open "where do you usually visit?"; Q6–9 convenience 4-point for work, medical supplies, food supplies, "recreational activities and see the doctor" – double-barrelled); R4A profile (70–74 = 50%; >10 years in Melbourne 45%; F 50/M 50); R4B medical supplies convenient 45% / inconvenient 27% / very inconvenient 28%; food convenient 83% / very convenient 17%; recreation & doctor inconvenient 70% / very inconvenient 30%; R4C 4 trams: lighting good/moderate/poor/good; reserved seating Y/Y/N/Y; wheelchair access present/not/not/present; space open/cramped/cramped/open.
- (a) Explain one weakness and one strength of the students' hypothesis. [4] – IVs (quality, accessibility) clearly defined & researchable; but whole CBD network too big for 4 students in 3 h; two dimensions too large a scope.

[5] – 4-point scale gives range; separate items (medical/work/food/recreation); open-ended locations; but no criteria for scale (subjective), Q9 double-barrelled, "food supplies" ambiguous (cooked food vs groceries).
