## H1 Paper 1 (8834/01), 31 Aug 2026
**Q1 Fieldwork – Beverage Container Return Scheme ("Return Right") participation by age, Bukit Panjang.** 6 students; aim: compare youths' participation with older age groups. Hypothesis: "Youths are more inclined to participate in the Beverage Container Return Scheme than other age groups." Counted people (incl. youths) returning containers at 3 of the neighbourhood's return points, 8–11am on one Friday, 3 groups concurrently; also questionnaires with youths at return points who agreed. R1A map of 3 return points X, Y, Z; R1B photos (X = Bukit Panjang Hawker Centre & Market; Y = FairPrice outlet, Saujana Rd; Z = HDB void deck, Senja Rd); R2 counts 8–11am (youth/total: X 18/36, Y 7/28, Z 10/24); R3 questionnaire (gender; age bands 0–7, 10–16, 20–25, >25 – gaps 8–9, 17–19; BP resident?; education; Q1 which age group more likely to use return points (perception); Q2 what you do with empty bottles; Q3 ever returned; Q4 reasons e.g. 10-cent deposit; Q5 what would encourage more returns).
- (a) Suggest how the students' hypothesis can be improved. [3] – add spatial scope (Bukit Panjang); define precise age ranges for "youths"/"other age groups" → consistent classification across observers.
- (b) With ref to R1A & R1B, explain one strength and one limitation of the selected return points. [4] – land-use diversity (hawker centre/supermarket/void deck); high footfall → larger sample (MS gives strengths only).
- (c) Suggest how students could minimise the impact of their investigation at the three return points. [4] – don't obstruct machines/walkways; approach only after returning; quiet, willing participants only; leave no litter.
- (d) Describe how the data shown in R2 may be represented. [4] – compound bar: X/Y/Z on x-axis, totals 36/28/24, split youth vs non-youth (18/18, 7/21, 10/14), legend/title.
- (e) Explain how the questionnaire in R3 may be improved. [5] – exhaustive, mutually exclusive age bands (0–9, 10–19 ...); survey all age groups not just youths; Q1 measures perception not behaviour → ask number of returns last month.
- (f) Evaluate the data collection methods for this investigation. [10] – concurrent 3-hr single Friday; observer consistency in judging age; site representativeness; voluntary/convenience sample; youth-only survey can't test comparison; % vs raw counts; repeat on other days.

**Q2 Urban reimaging – Stratford / London 2012 Olympics (East London).** R4A Warton Rd, Stratford 2005 (weathered brick industrial wall, dumped fridges, debris) vs R4B 2012 (modern multi-storey building with colourful panels, security booth, barriers, bollards); R5 bar graph land use East London 2005/2012/2015 (ha): derelict/industrial 160→20→5; sports venues 0→65→45; housing 10→25→55; business/commercial 20→35→50; parks/open space 25→65→~100; transport ~25→35→38; R6 maps Olympic Park 2012 (venues, Olympic Village, broadcast/media centres) vs 2015 (East Village, Chobham Manor, East Wick, Here East business, Westfield, VeloPark, Copper Box, Olympic Stadium).
- (a) Explain the economic reasons for urban reimaging efforts. [4] – counter deindustrialisation; tourism revenue; attract FDI (e.g. Jurong Lake District >S$10bn investment).
- (b) Using R4A & R4B, describe the changes to the built environment along Warton Road, 2005–2012. [3] – building design; cleanliness; access/security infrastructure.
- (c) Explain how the changes in R4A & R4B may improve the liveability of Stratford. [5] – economic (attract business → jobs, services); environmental (bulky waste removed, hygiene); social (road safety).
- (d) With ref to R5 & R6, explain how the London 2012 Olympic Games has contributed to SUD. [6] – economic (business land 20→50 ha; media centre → Here East); environmental (derelict 160→5 ha; parks 25→100 ha); social (housing 10→55 ha; East Village, Chobham Manor, East Wick).
- (e) Using named example(s), suggest why urban reimaging strategies may be unsuccessful. [4] – Rio 2016 Olympic Park underused, vandalism (Maracanã, Aquatics Stadium); Palm Jumeirah dredging/reclamation, altered water circulation.

**Q3 Climate change – SST anomalies, aquatic ecosystems, sea-level rise.** R7 global SST anomalies March 2024 (Copernicus ERA5, vs 1991–2020): mostly positive; highest (+2 to >+3°C) in equatorial/N Atlantic, E tropical Pacific, parts of Indian Ocean; small negative patches S Pacific/Southern Ocean; R8 article (adapted IPCC AR6 WGII 2022): marine heatwaves → coral bleaching; acidification harms shell-formers/plankton → food chains; nutrient changes; degraded reefs reduce wave-energy buffering → storm surge exposure; R9 NOAA graph (Sweet et al. 2022) sea-level pathways to 2100 above 2000 levels: low ~1 ft, intermediate-low ~1.6, intermediate ~3.3, intermediate-high ~5, high ~6.7 ft.
- (a) Using R7, describe global SST anomalies in March 2024. [4] – widespread positive; hotspots equatorial Atlantic/E Pacific/Indian; limited negatives S Pacific/Southern Ocean; not spatially uniform.
- (b) Suggest how contemporary CC may accelerate warming through positive feedbacks. [3] – ice-albedo (Arctic sea ice); permafrost thaw releasing CH4/CO2.
- (c) With ref to R8, explain how changes in aquatic ecosystems as a result of contemporary CC can affect humans. [7] – fish catches/livelihoods; food-chain collapse → commercial species; coastal erosion/flooding; reef tourism (diving) revenue.
- (d) Using R9, compare possible pathways for future sea level rise under different GHG emissions scenarios. [4] – all rise, accelerate late century, none stabilise; high ~6.7 ft vs low ~1 ft by 2100 (max 2 if only similarities/differences).
- (e) Suggest reasons why projections of future sea-level rise remain uncertain. [4] – future emissions (population, economy, policy); poorly understood ice-sheet dynamics/feedbacks.

**Q2 Tropical cyclone Narelle; ENSO SST anomalies (Cluster 2).** R6 track & intensity of TC Narelle, 17–28 Mar 2026: forms Coral Sea (~155°E, ~12°S) 17 Mar, moves W across N Australia/Gulf of Carpentaria (Cat 1 → Cat 3–4 ~19–20 Mar), weakens to tropical storm over Top End/Arafura–Timor Seas (21–24), re-intensifies off W Australia (Cat 3–4 ~25–26 Mar), turns S along coast and dissipates inland (~28 Mar); R7A SST anomaly E Pacific Nov 2025 (cool, negative anomalies near Equator/Central America, to ~−4/−5°C); R7B May 2026 (strong warm anomalies +3 to >+5°C along Equator and S American coast).

## H2 Paper 2 (9173/02), 17 Sep 2026
**Q1 Fieldwork – Reusable bag initiative at supermarkets as CC response (Singapore neighbourhood).** 5 students, one Sunday afternoon. Hypothesis: "Supermarket shoppers in Singapore are likely to bring their own reusable bags when charged a fee for disposable carrier bags." Outlet A charges $0.05/bag, Outlet B does not; 2 groups concurrently near checkout counters counting shoppers & reusable-bag users; photographed shoppers at checkout; 5 neighbourhood residents recruited via family members for an online survey. R1 map: A next to MRT station + bus stop 10111 on main road/park connector; B in a Neighbourhood Centre among HDB blocks (bus stops ~100 m); R2A photo at A (BYOB poster, cashier, identifiable shopper); R2B photo at B (identifiable cashier, elderly shopper, plastic bags); R3 online survey form (name, mobile no., age, address; (i) bring reusable bags Y/N; (ii) motivations; (iii) influence of discount/reward points; (iv) influence of extra charge; (v) most effective strategy (4 options, no "Others"); "(v)(i)" open-ended why); R4 data 1–3pm: A 52 shoppers, 16 disposable, 36 reusable (69.2%); B 38 shoppers, 19/19 (50.0%).
- (a) Suggest why the hypothesis is appropriate for the investigation. [4] – clear IV (fee) and DV (bring reusable bags); measurable/testable by counts.
- (b) Using R1, explain the advantages of choosing the two supermarket outlets for data collection. [4] – accessible; transport links → footfall → larger sample.
- (c) With ref to R2A, R2B & R3, explain possible ethical issues arising from the students' data collection process. [6] – no consent for photos of identifiable shoppers/staff; survey lacks purpose/right to withdraw; personal data (name, phone, address) → no anonymity.
- (d) With ref to R3, explain two strengths and one limitation of the online survey form. [6] – standardised options; quant + qual (open question); Q(v) restrictive, no "Others"/"none".
- (e) Explain how the students could improve their sampling method for the online survey. [6] – larger than 5; probability sampling instead of family recruitment; more neighbourhoods.
- (f) Describe how the data in R4 may be represented. [4] – compound bar per outlet: A 16/36, B 19/19; axes, legend, title.
- (g) With ref to all the Resources, evaluate the validity of the students' fieldwork. [10] – MS: GLDs only.
