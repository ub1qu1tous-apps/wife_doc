## H1 Paper 1 (8834/01), 19 Aug 2026
**Q1 Fieldwork – Perceptions of sustainable healthy diets, Fremantle (port city, Australia).** 6 students; FAO/WHO (2019) definition of sustainable healthy diets quoted. Hypothesis: "Adults living in Fremantle with higher educational attainment (above pre-university level) are more likely to be concerned about sustainable healthy diet practices." Questionnaire to 100 residents aged 18–75 at two sites, one Friday 11am–2pm, convenience sampling; 2 residents also gave face-to-face interviews. R1 Google map of Site A (Fremantle Markets) and Site B (John Curtin College of the Arts); R2 photos of sites; R3 sample by education: secondary & below 24, JC equivalent 31, diploma 10, bachelor's 26, master's 8, doctorate 1 (n = 100); R4 questionnaire (Sec A demographics: resident?, age 18–25/26–40/41–60/61–75, education; Sec B: Q1 heard of term; Q2 associations tick-all; Q3 self-rated understanding 5-pt; Q4 Likert (a)–(e) (environment, health, too expensive, easy in Fremantle, individuals make difference); Q5 top-3 food factors; Q6 willingness to change habits); R5 summary %: age 18–25 18, 26–40 37, 41–60 31, 61–75 14; Q1 yes 40/no 60; Q3 very good 20, good 22, average 18, poor 25, very poor 15; Q4(a) 4–5 = 90%; Q4(c) agree (4–5) 50%; Q4(d) 4–5 = 40% (results not broken down by education).
- (a) Explain why the students' hypothesis is appropriate for the investigation. [3] – specific target group (adults in Fremantle); term defined via FAO/WHO; suitable scale.
- (b) With ref to R1, R2 & R3, suggest how the students could have obtained a more representative sample of residents in Fremantle. [4] – survey more varied locations (residential areas, workplaces, community centres) not just a market and a school; vary timing (weekday 11–2 under-represents workers).
- (c) With ref to R2, suggest how the students could minimise potential risks when conducting the survey. [4] – work in pairs/trios; seek permission from market / college management.
- (d) With ref to R4, explain the strengths and limitations of the questionnaire. [4] – S: logical two-section structure; L: social desirability bias (Q6 "very willing"). Max 2 if one side; questionnaire only, not sampling.
- (e) Sketch a graph to present the data for Q3 in R5 and explain its suitability. [5] – pie chart (20/22/18/25/15) or bar graph; title, labelled sectors, legend, accuracy + 2m justification.

**Q2 Waterfront redevelopment / reimaging – Shanghai (Huangpu River).** R6 map of 4 projects along Huangpu (Yangpu Waterfront, Lujiazui Financial & Trade Zone, 2010 World Expo Site, Xuhui West Bund; Pudong district shaded); R7 table: Yangpu – 2002, 12.9 km², industrial/residential/transport nodes, entrepreneurship, adaptation & regeneration, goal urban innovative district; Lujiazui – 1990, 28.0 km², industrial/residential/agricultural, special economic zones, new CBD/financial centre; World Expo – 2002, 6.7 km², industrial/residential, mega event, HQ cluster & business centre; Xuhui West Bund – 2010, 9.4 km², industrial/former airport, culture-led regeneration, cultural & commercial cluster; R8 photos Pudong 1987 (flat, low-rise industrial, docks) vs 2013 (dense iconic towers; Bund foreground unchanged).
- (a) Using R6 & R7, compare the four waterfront redevelopment projects in Shanghai. [5] – all had industrial land; all regenerative; differ in size (28 vs 6.7 km²), timing (1990 → 2010), strategy (SEZ, mega-event, innovation, culture-led). Max 3 for one side only.
- (b) Suggest reasons for the different development strategies used. [4] – timing: early-1990s liberalisation → SEZ/FDI; by 2002 diversification; Expo opportunistic funding & visibility.
- (c) With ref to R8, compare the financial district of Pudong before and after the redevelopment. [4] – similar: Huangpu River & river traffic; Bund colonial buildings (Custom House clock tower); different: industrial waterfront → high-rise financial skyline.
- (d) Account for the varying success of urban reimaging strategies across cities. [5] – finance; political will/governance (Singapore, Barcelona); pre-existing reputation; fit of strategy to local context (culture-led needs creative base).
- (e) Explain the demand placed on natural environments by urban areas. [4] – land conversion/habitat loss/fragmentation; water abstraction; waste & pollution beyond city boundary.

**Q3 Climate change – indicators over 2,000 years; precipitation change at 2°C.** R9 (Climate Lab Book) indicators year 1–2024 CE: CO2 ~280 ppm until ~1750 then to 424 ppm (2024); temperature reconstructed ~0°C then observed rise to 1.3°C; rate of sea-level rise ~0 then to 3.6 cm/decade; "invention of steam engine" marked; R10 IPCC AR6 WG1 SPM Fig 5.2: simulated annual mean precipitation change at 2°C vs 1850–1900 (wetter high latitudes, equatorial Pacific, Sahara/N Africa, S Asia; drier Mediterranean, Central America/Caribbean, Amazon/S America, southern Africa, SW/SE Australia).
- (a) Using R9, describe the indicators of global climate change over the last 2024 years. [4] – stable most of period then sharp rise after ~1750/1850; CO2 275–280 → 424 ppm; temp → +1.3°C; SLR rate ~0 → 3.6 cm/decade.
- (b) Explain why atmospheric CO2 levels are increasing. [4] – fossil fuel combustion; deforestation (lost sinks + burning); agriculture.
- (c) Using R10, describe the simulated annual mean precipitation changes. [4] – uneven; +10–30% high latitudes; +10–40% equatorial Pacific/N Africa; −10–20% Mediterranean; ~−10% southern Africa, Australia, Central/S America.
- (d) Suggest possible reasons for the changes in R10. [5] – warmer air holds ~7% more moisture per °C; poleward storm-track shift; intensified convection in tropics; warmer equatorial Pacific SST.
- (e) Describe how the impact of climate change on aquatic and terrestrial ecosystems may affect humans. [5] – coral bleaching/acidification → reef fisheries, food security; pollinator decline → crop yields; desertification → displacement. Max 3 if one ecosystem type.

- (a) Explain one strength and one weakness of the students' hypothesis. [4] – S: clear, researchable, two defined age groups; W: assumes age is the main determinant (ignores income, education, household type).
- (b) Using R1A, R1B & R2, explain why the site chosen is suitable. [4] – central hub with high resident footfall; mature estate with both age cohorts; ethnic mix (per stem) reduces bias.
- (c) With ref to R1A & R1B, suggest how the students could minimise the impact of their investigation. [4] – stay clear of MRT/bus interchange & mall entrances; seek permission from AMK Hub management.
