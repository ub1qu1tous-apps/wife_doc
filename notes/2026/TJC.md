## H1 Paper 1 (8834/01), 31 Aug 2026
**Q1 Fieldwork – Community recycling, Toa Payoh (mature HDB estate).** 20 students; recycling "as a response to climate change and resource scarcity". RQ: 'To what extent does the accessibility of recycling facilities influence correct recycling practices in Toa Payoh?' 8 sites chosen on a base map; at each, nearest blue bin, visual audit (no touching) of the first 20 clearly visible top-layer items as correct recyclables vs contamination; in pairs, 4–6pm, one Tuesday in July; same questionnaire to 80 people – approached anyone who said they lived in Toa Payoh. R1 base map of Toa Payoh with sites A–H; R2A photo of top layer of a blue bin (bottles, cans, food-soiled containers, paper); R2B students surveying on a shared covered walkway (cyclist passing); R3 questionnaire (Q1 frequency regularly/occasionally/rarely/never; Q2 single main barrier; Q3 tick accepted items incl. glass, food scraps, styrofoam, e-waste; Q4 walking time <2 / 2–5 / >5 min / don't know; Q5 'easy to see and reach' scale 1,2,3,5,6; Q6 open); R4A audit results (correct/20: A Toa Payoh Central commercial 11, B bus interchange 12, C Lor 1 res 15, D Lor 2 res 17, E Lor 4 market 9, F Lor 5 res 13, G Lor 7 res 16, H Lor 8 market 8); R4B survey (regular 31, occasional 29, rarely/never 20; barriers: rinse 24, unsure 21, inconvenient 17, other 18; all-correct identification 46; >5 min or don't know 25; agree easy to reach 49).
- (a) Explain why the research question is appropriate for this investigation. [3] – measurable relationship; walking time/perception = access, audit = correct recycling; 8 sites allow spatial comparison.
- (b) With reference to Resources 2A and 2B, suggest how the students could minimise potential risks during the investigation. [5] – no handling, sharp items, sanitise (2A); pairs, side of walkway, pedestrians/cyclists (2B); risk without precaution = 0.
- (c) With reference to Resource 3, explain the limitations of the survey questionnaire when used to survey residents during the investigation. [4] – vague frequency words, estimated walk time, Q2 only one barrier, Q5 double-barrelled + misnumbered scale, self-report ≠ behaviour.
- (d) Using Resource 4A, describe the extent of correct recycling practices in Toa Payoh. [4] – 101/160 ≈ 63% correct; correct > contaminants at 6/8 sites; D 17/20 vs H 8/20; residential > market/food.
- (e) With reference to Resource 4B, use a suitable data representation technique to show the main barriers to recycling identified by respondents. [4] – bar graph, zero-based scale, title/labels, accurate plotting.
- (f) With reference to all the resources, evaluate the reliability and accuracy of the investigation. [10] – common questionnaire & consistent 20-item audit help; one day, 8 sites, top layer only, self-report, estimated walking times; neither resource proves causation; improve with more sites/times, measured walking times.

- (c) Using Resource 9, compare the sources of greenhouse gas emissions in UK and Ethiopia in 2023. [4]
- (d) Using Resource 10, describe the predicted changes to the extent of permafrost in northern Eurasia and North America. [6] – shrinks from southern margins northward 2050→2100; Russia, Alaska, Canada; fragmentation; remains in far north.

## H2 Paper 2 (9173/02), 15 Sep 2026
**Q1 Fieldwork – Community responses to CC mitigation, Nottingham UK.** 8 students; Clifton South (post-war housing, council-led energy retrofitting) vs The Park Estate (older, higher-value housing, conservation controls). RQ: "How do community responses to climate-change mitigation vary between Clifton South and The Park Estate in Nottingham?" One Saturday in June: visual audit from public pavements along one transect per area, 5 sampling sites (A–E) each; questionnaire to 60 residents per area 11:00–15:00, at the busiest public location near each transect, every third passer-by who appeared ≥18. R1 transect maps (Clifton South A–E along a footpath; Park Estate A–E unevenly spaced along Derby Rd/Tennis Dr); R2 photos (2A solar panels on Clifton South semis; 2B Newcastle Drive, Victorian villas on a slope); R3 audit table (buildings observed, solar, insulation, bike parking, EV points, recycling, LED lights per site; e.g. Clifton South D 24 bldgs/12 solar/11 insulation; Park Estate B 28 bldgs/2 solar/1 insulation but more bike parking, e.g. PE D 12, EV 6); R4 questionnaire (profile: area, age, residence length, tenure; Likert Q3–5 attitudes; Q6–9 frequency always…never; Q10 two reasons; Q11 one barrier; Q12 effectiveness; Q13 open); R5A mean attitude scores CS vs PE: serious problem 4.5/4.4, household action helps 4.0/3.8, council provides enough 3.6/2.8; R5B % using public transport instead of car: never 10/25, rarely 10/20, sometimes 20/25, often 35/20, always 25/10.
- (a) Explain one strength and one limitation of the students' research question. [3] – focused/measurable vs "community responses" undefined.
- (b) With reference to Resources 1 and 2, explain how the students could minimise potential risks and address ethical concerns when conducting the investigation. [5] – road crossings, steep ground, traffic; consent, anonymity, don't enter property/photograph residents.
- (c) With reference to Resource 3, explain the limitations of the sampling method used by the students for the visual audit. [5] – 5 sites/one transect, uneven spacing, unclear basis; unequal building counts (use %); only visible measures.
- (d) Explain two other sources of secondary data that could help students achieve their investigation aim. [2] – council retrofit/planning records, census, energy-use data.
- (e) Explain the strengths and weaknesses of the survey questionnaire shown in Resource 4. [5] – consistent scales, sections, open Q; loaded/double-barrelled Q3, undefined frequency terms, Q7 assumes car use, Q12 unmeasurable.
- (f) Using Resource 5A, compare residents' attitudes to climate-change mitigation in Clifton South and The Park Estate. [5]
- (g) Using Resource 5B, sketch a suitable graph to compare the residents' mitigation action in Clifton South and The Park Estate. [5] – grouped bar graph.
- (h) With reference to all the resources, evaluate the validity of the students' fieldwork. [10] – one transect, one day, residency unverified, unequal counts; triangulate, use %.
